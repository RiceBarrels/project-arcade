## 3. How it works

**Section title:** How it works

1. **Browse** — Find gear in the catalogue: tools, cameras, camping kit, games, instruments and more. Filter by category and area.
2. **Queue it** — Add what you want to your queue. If it's out on loan, you keep your place in line. When it's free, you're next.
3. **Borrow** — The lender approves your request and you agree a handoff time and place. Take it, use it, enjoy it.
4. **Return** — Bring it back in the condition you got it. The lender marks it returned and the next person in line is up.

**Closing line:** Got something gathering dust? [List it](/list) and let someone else get some use out of it.

## 6. Damage price explainer

**Title:** What's a damage price?

Every listing has a damage price. It's what the borrower agrees to owe the lender if the item comes back damaged or lost.

- **Lenders** set it when listing. Make it fair: a repair cost or replacement value.
- **Borrowers** see it before requesting, so there are no surprises.
- **If something goes wrong,** the lender marks the item as damaged when it's returned, and the damage price applies.
- **Payment is between the two of you.** The site doesn't collect or hold money and can't force a payment. Talk it through, be reasonable, and settle it directly.

Normal wear and tear isn't damage. When in doubt, take photos at handoff and at return.

**Short version (for tooltips/checkout step):** Damage price: what you'd owe the lender if this comes back damaged or lost. Settled directly between you two.

## 6c. FAQ: damage price

**Who sets the damage price?**
The lender does, when they list the item. You'll see it on the listing before you request, so you always know it up front.

**Who collects the money? Does the site take payment?**
No. The site never collects, holds or passes on money. If an item is marked as damaged or lost, the borrower and lender settle the damage price directly between them. The site can't force a payment, so keep it friendly and be reasonable.

**How do I set a fair price?**
Use what it would cost to repair or replace the item, whichever is more realistic. If it's an older item, consider what a comparable used one costs. Setting it too high puts borrowers off; too low and you're out of pocket if something goes wrong. A short note on the item's condition in the listing helps both sides.

**What happens if nothing gets damaged?**
Nothing. The damage price is only owed if the lender marks the item as damaged or lost on return. If it comes back fine, the lender marks it returned OK and nobody pays anything. Normal wear and tear isn't damage.
